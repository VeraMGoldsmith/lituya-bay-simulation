Question and scope

How does the apparent sensitivity to the numerical water-speed limit depend on the reported quantity and the measurement definition?

This preliminary analysis uses published summaries for session comparison_runs/20261019T041033Z_ff1b17c2, covering 420 s. Only the 50 and 100 m/s cases passed the recorded basic checks. The 200 m/s case remains excluded because it failed the recorded CFL/time-step checks.

Measurement definitions

Run-up is the highest eligible ground elevation reached by at least 0.1 m of water. Eligibility requires an observed level-1 monitor cell, ground elevation greater than zero, and exclusion of cells with any positive bilinear overlap with artificial geometry or an enabled virtual wall. The monitoring terrain is static. These masks and definitions are inherited from the archived measurement code, not recomputed or changed here.

Wetted land means eligible land wetted at any time during the recorded period, not simultaneous inundation. The expanded monitor records 1267 and 1412 wetted eligible cells. The fixed spacing is 30.0 × 29.96688741721846 m, giving 899.006622516553800 m² per cell. Area is count × dx × dy / 1,000,000. Spacing is checked against the domain and grid dimensions; no 30 × 30 m approximation is used.

Gauge values are simulated peak water-surface elevations, not historical observations or peak-to-trough wave heights. Recorded sampling ranges: cap_050 (P2, P3, P5): 0.0–419.7055 s; cap_100 (P2, P3, P5): 0.0–419.6951 s. The report uses those recorded samples without extrapolation to the final field time; per-gauge sample counts and peak times are retained in provenance.

Monitoring-area choice

Local monitoring bounds: original (legacy): X = 900.0–2500.0 m, Y = 900.0–2300.0 m; expanded: X = -1000.0–3000.0 m, Y = 0.0–2300.0 m. Comparisons between speed limits use the same area, threshold and eligibility rules. The original area is contained in the expanded area: these are overlapping coverage choices, not independent replications.

The cap-related run-up increase persists in both areas: 124.27 m (36.58%) in the original area and 130.12 m (38.30%) in the expanded area. Expanding coverage changes the 50 m/s maximum by 0.00 m and the 100 m/s maximum by 5.85 m (1.26%).

Recorded boundary-touch flags: cap_050: original yes, expanded no; cap_100: original yes, expanded no. The additional coverage changes the 100 m/s measurement slightly, but does not remove the difference between settings. This is not evidence of a bay-wide maximum.

Supported wet-depth-threshold bound

For both cases, the recorded expanded-area run-up is unchanged for every reporting threshold from 0.1 through 1.0 m, conditional on the archived evidence and fixed eligible cells.

Raising the threshold can only remove qualifying cells, so the maximum ground elevation cannot increase. A cell attaining the existing maximum remains qualified if its archived maximum depth reaches the new threshold. At least one such surviving cell gives the same lower bound, proving equality. The witness depths come specifically from the run-up-maximizing peak_locations entries, cross-checked between results and figure provenance, not the maximum depth elsewhere on land.

This is a set-inclusion deduction from archived peak-cell information, not a newly executed threshold sweep. The published provenance records prior raw-monitor remeasurement and static-bed verification; this reanalysis verifies those records and source hashes but does not independently recheck the raw fields. It establishes neither unchanged wetted area nor behavior outside the interval, grid convergence, or physical accuracy.

Interpretation and limits

The response depends on the reported quantity. Expanded-area run-up changes by 38.30%, wetted land area by 11.44%, and maximum land depth by 5.87%. P2 has equal recorded peaks; P3 changes by 6.46%, and P5 by -1.70%. Equal P2 peaks do not establish identical time series. The small decrease at P5 complicates a blanket claim that every response increases with the speed limit.

This is a preliminary analysis of one configuration. Neither accepted speed limit is established as physically correct or optimal. The results do not establish historical accuracy or grid convergence. Artificial-boundary effects remain despite exclusion of artificial cells from measurements. Reanalysis of published summaries is distinct from independently reproducing the underlying simulations.
